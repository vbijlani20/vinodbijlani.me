**Every task we automate changes more than productivity. It changes how people learn, make decisions and take responsibility, and how work flows through the organisation.**

Those questions shaped the AI Workforce Redesign Hackathon in Sydney on 16 October 2026, hosted by Humanity in Business, where I joined as a judge. Ten teams of five worked from 8 am to 4 pm on workforce challenges, using design thinking, and presented their proposed solutions. Each team showed how differently the same challenge could be approached.

A few ideas stayed with me:

- **Set the constraints aside first.** Explore what the work could become before narrowing the options, then bring real constraints back to test feasibility.
- **Redesign the whole process.** Map the tasks, decisions and handoffs. If AI accelerates one step, what happens downstream? Review, approvals and exception handling need to evolve too.
- **Rethink the operating model.** Who owns the workflow? What can an agent decide? When must a person intervene? Define decision rights, accountability and how teams work together.
- **Prepare people to lead agents.** Prompting is only the beginning. Employees need to direct, monitor and validate agents, with the authority to act.
- **Protect the path to expertise.** If AI handles entry-level tasks, graduates need new ways to build judgement: simulations, supervised decisions and customer exposure.
- **Give freed capacity a purpose.** Better service, deeper analysis and new opportunities, supported by reskilling, shared incentives and measures that recognise outcomes.

My takeaway: **the AI roadmap, the workforce plan and the operating model need to be designed together.**
