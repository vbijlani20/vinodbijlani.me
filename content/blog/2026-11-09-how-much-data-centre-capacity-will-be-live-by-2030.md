**More than 100 GW of new data centre capacity is planned across just six markets.** How much will actually come online by 2030?

My last posts mapped operational capacity across 20 markets as of June 2026. This time I looked at the buildout ahead, and how much of it is real.

**The two giants.** The United States goes from about 48 GW today to 114–118 GW by 2030 (BNEF): 66 to 70 GW more. China goes from about 32 GW to about 60 GW (Rystad): around 28 GW more.

**The pipelines that stand out:**

- **Malaysia:** 8.5 GW in Knight Frank's pipeline, and that is Johor alone.
- **India:** an 8.3 GW pipeline against 1.6 GW live, with nearly two-thirds at an early stage.
- **Australia:** about 23 GW announced, but CBA puts the feasible pipeline at about 7 GW.
- **Finland:** a pipeline nearly ten times its current footprint.

These are pipelines, not delivery schedules. Many projects have no confirmed completion date, and the figures cover both AI and conventional workloads.

**The gap between announced and energised is already showing.** The IEA says around 20% of planned data centre projects are at risk of grid delays. In Europe, the pipeline implies about 130% growth, while the IEA models about 70% by 2030. Bloomberg reports that a third to a half of US capacity planned for 2026 has been delayed or cancelled. And Microsoft recently walked away from a UK AI site after its grid connection slipped into the 2030s.

Power, transformers, financing, local opposition and the real pace of AI demand will decide the final number.
