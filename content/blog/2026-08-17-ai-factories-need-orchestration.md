**AI transformation is not just about GPUs and models. It is also about the infrastructure underneath them.** That was one of my biggest takeaways from the customer panel at SUSE Summit Sydney, where we discussed how virtualisation and containers are becoming building blocks for enterprise AI, and what it takes to move AI from pilots into production.

**1. AI factories need orchestration, not just infrastructure.** GPUs, storage and networking are only the foundation. The real value comes from orchestrating the compute, models, data, containers, applications and security policies across the environment.

**2. AI workloads will not live in one place.** Some will run in the data centre, some in private cloud, some at the edge. The platform needs to make that complexity invisible to the teams building and consuming AI.

**3. Virtual machines and containers will coexist.** Enterprise AI is not a clean-sheet environment. Organisations have existing applications, virtualised workloads and legacy infrastructure alongside new Kubernetes-native AI workloads. The goal is not to rip everything out. It is to create a common operating model across both.

**4. Successful AI adoption is ultimately about business outcomes.** The customer stories reinforced it: AI becomes valuable when it moves beyond experimentation and starts solving real operational problems reliably, securely and at scale.

Thanks to Linus Lai (IDC), Lynn Soe (IAG) and Chris Ford (Aussie Broadband) for the discussion, and to Ben Henshall for facilitating it.

The AI factory is the foundation. **Orchestration is what turns that foundation into an operating AI platform**, and increasingly it is the control plane that connects compute, data, models, applications and governance. That is where I think the next wave of AI platform differentiation will happen.
