I have been tracking Asia's AI infrastructure buildout, and the numbers are getting hard to ignore: **more than $460 billion of investment, 240+ projects and 19.4 GW already operational.**

Asia's AI factory capacity could reach **71 to 84 GW by 2030**. That is roughly a fivefold increase in less than five years.

**China** remains the anchor, moving from around 12.5 GW today towards 30 GW or more by 2030. **India** is moving particularly fast, from around 1.6 GW towards 12 to 14 GW, with Reliance, Adani, AWS, Microsoft and Tata all contributing to what is becoming a major AI infrastructure market. **Japan and South Korea** are investing heavily in sovereign AI infrastructure, with NTT, SoftBank, SK Group, Samsung and Naver building dedicated AI capacity. And **Southeast Asia** is becoming increasingly interesting, with Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines emerging as major markets.

But getting to 71 to 84 GW is not simply a question of building more data centres. **Power is going to be one of the biggest constraints.** And economics will determine how much of the planned capacity actually makes sense to build and operate.

This is where the AI factory conversation gets interesting. The question is not just how much compute we can install. It is how economically we can turn that compute and power into useful intelligence. What does a token cost? How efficiently are we using the GPUs? How much does inference cost at scale?

Then comes the other side of the equation: **AI security and governance.** What data can the AI access? Who can use the models? What can an agent actually do? How do we govern and secure the entire pipeline?

**Power, economics, security and governance will determine how effectively we can turn all that infrastructure into AI value.** That is the part of Asia's buildout I am watching most closely.
